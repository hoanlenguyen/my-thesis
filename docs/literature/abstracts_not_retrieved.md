### S10: Agentic AI home energy management system: A large language model framework for residential load scheduling

- **Triage:** Published version of El Makroum et al. (2025), which the thesis cites as an arXiv preprint: Results in Engineering 29, 109857 (2026). Update the reference.
- **Abstract (OpenAlex):** The electricity sector transition requires increased residential demand response capacity, yet Home Energy Management Systems (HEMS) adoption remains limited by user interaction barriers. Large language models (LLMs) offer potential to address these barriers through natural language interaction; however, existing implementations employ LLMs as rule generators, optimizers, or parameter extractors rather than autonomous coordinators for multi-appliance scheduling. ### I06: Integrating Large Language Models and Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Home Energy Management
- Yang Zhang, Chongyu Wang, Lindong Xie, Yunjian Xu, Edward Chung, Mohammad Shahidehpour et al. (2026). IEEE Internet of Things Journal. DOI: 10.1109/jiot.2026.3719299
- **Triage:** LLM as the decision actor in closed-loop HEMS scheduling, fine-tuned with PPO and LoRA; constrained action sampling keeps outputs to admissible appliance actions. Close to the thesis question of LLM scheduling with safety.
- **Abstract (Semantic Scholar):** The home energy management system (HEMS) has gained significant attention with the advancement of smart monitoring and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies. Data-driven approaches, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), have shown promise in learning HEMS scheduling policies through environment interaction but often suffer from poor sample efficiency and high computational overhead in environments with delayed rewards. To address these challenges, this article proposes a hybrid framework integrating large language models (LLMs) and RL for efficient and safe HEMS scheduling. Unlike existing LLM-based energy studies that mainly use LLMs for forecasting, knowledge processing, or open-loop advisory support, the proposed framework deploys an LLM as a direct decision-making actor in a closed-loop HEMS control setting. By leveraging the LLM’s pretrained reasoning capability as an implicit policy prior, the proposed method improves the sample efficiency of RL-based HEMS scheduling without requiring expert demonstrations. Proximal policy optimization (PPO)-based fine-tuning with low-rank adaptation (LoRA) further enables efficient LLM–HEMS alignment by updating only approximately 0.1% of the model parameters. Moreover, a constrained action-sampling mechanism restricts LLM outputs to predefined admissible appliance actions, thus ensuring physically executable, constraint-compliant scheduling decisions. Experiments driven by real-world market and appliance data show that the proposed method improves home utility by 33.3%, reduces user discomfort costs by 28.6%, and requires over 20 times fewer training episodes than conventional RL baselines.

### S03: Integrating large language models into Peer-to-Peer energy management for multi-tenant buildings: A guardrail approach to ensuring resilience
- Vangipuram Srinivasa Raghavan, Giridhar Archakam Vijayaraghavulu (2026). Neural Networks. DOI: 10.1016/j.neunet.2026.108814
- **Triage:** Planner-Supervisor-Executor HEMS for P2P markets: LLM plans, a deterministic supervisor validates, MILP and MPC execute. Directly comparable to the verification gate and optimiser tool of the thesis.
- **Abstract (Semantic Scholar):** While the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into Home Energy Management Systems (HEMS) promises intuitive interfaces, their inherent unreliability poses a significant risk to the stability of Peer-to-Peer (P2P) energy markets. This paper proposes and validates a Planner-Supervisor-Executor architecture that ensures resilient operation through a deterministic guardrail. A benchmark of multiple planner models confirmed that while open-source models (Phi-3 mini, Gemma 7B) and a keyword baseline consistently failed on complex, multi-action commands, Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash achieved the highest workflow accuracy at 89.19%, proving its superior capability in interpreting nuanced user intent. The system's backend couples a day-ahead Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) scheduler with a real-time Model Predictive Control (MPC) dispatcher to manage stochastic solar generation and optimize community trading via a dynamic P2P pricing mechanism, with fairness validated by the Shapley value and Nash Bargaining Solution. Operational safety is enforced through a three-stage validation process. An initial LLM-based semantic guardrail screens for illogical commands prior to workflow generation, the resulting plan is then verified by the deterministic MARCO supervisor, which effectively intercepts unsafe or malformed workflows. Under these safeguards, the proposed system demonstrates substantial cost savings, increases energy self-sufficiency by up to 64% during the monsoon season, and enables safe "what-if" analyses for residents. Overall, this work provides a validated blueprint for decoupling probabilistic LLMs from deterministic control, proving that robust supervision is a prerequisite for safely deploying AI in critical cyber-physical systems.

### S07: What LLM-Simulated Users Can and Cannot Tell Us About Conversational Energy Management Systems
- Wooyoung Jung (2026). . DOI: 10.1145/3765611.3815484
- **Triage:** First empirical assessment of LLM-simulated users for evaluating conversational HEMS; relevant to the limitations and evaluation discussion (Michelon et al. and HEMA use simulated users).
- **Abstract (Semantic Scholar):** Conversational Home Energy Management Systems (HEMS), capable of understanding and responding to natural language commands on home energy management (HEM) tasks, introduce new evaluation challenges since users can request any information or action in free-form language. Consequently, the Large Language Model (LLM)-as-simulated-user methodology, where LLMs role-play as users to evaluate conversational systems, is emerging in HEMS research. However, growing evidence from the Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) community indicates that LLM-simulated users are unreliable proxies for real humans – they are systematically more cooperative, inflate system success rates, and fail to reproduce demographic-specific interaction patterns. This study reframes the value of the LLM-as-simulated-user methodology for HEMS research by hypothesizing that it can still function as a system diagnostic tool. To test this hypothesis, this study conducts the first empirical assessment of the LLM-as-simulated-user methodology in the HEMS domain, analyzing 105 conversational logs between a multi-agent HEMS and LLM-simulated users (three user personas × seven HEM scenarios × five repetitions). This analysis assesses this evaluation methodology across multiple validity dimensions, including reproducibility, scenario discrimination, diagnostic utility, simulated user cooperativeness and persona fidelity, and deployability. Results show that this methodology produces reproducible pass/fail verdicts (90.5% goal-outcome consistency across repeated trials), discriminates system capability boundaries across task complexity levels, and surfaces actionable defects including factual errors and interaction weaknesses. However, consistent with prior literature, simulated users exhibit politeness in 96% of conversations (vs. 20% for real humans) and show weak persona fidelity (1.8 of 4 defined behaviors present on average, validated by human annotation with Cohen’s κ = 0.83). These findings provide evidence-based guidance for HEMS researchers on the appropriate use of the LLM-as-simulated-user methodology and contribute to the broader discourse on LLM evaluation validity in HCI in domain-specific applications.
